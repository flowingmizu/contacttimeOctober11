Is the water safe yet?

Your water has been through coagulation, settling and filtration. Now it needs disinfection, and chlorine needs time to work. Operators prove it with one number.

Why some water leaves early

A clearwell is not a queue. Without baffles, some water short-circuits from inlet to outlet and gets far less contact time than the average. Release a pulse of tracer dye and watch.

T10 and the baffling factor

T10 is the time it takes for the first 10% of water to pass through the basin. Regulators use T10, not the average, so that almost all water gets at least that much contact.

T10 = HDT × baffling factor

HDT (hydraulic detention time) = volume ÷ flow. Real baffling factors come from tracer studies; the EPA guidance values are:

ConditionFactorDescription
Unbaffled0.1No baffles, agitated, high inlet/outlet velocity
Poor0.3Single or multiple unbaffled inlets and outlets
Average0.5Baffled inlet or outlet, some intra-basin baffles
Superior0.7Perforated inlet baffle, serpentine baffles
Perfect1.0Plug flow, e.g. a long pipeline

Remember titration? pH decides how strong your chlorine is

Free chlorine splits into HOCl (hypochlorous acid, the strong disinfectant) and OCl⁻ (hypochlorite, roughly 80 times weaker). The higher the pH, the more of it is OCl⁻.

HOCl OCl⁻

Cold water slows chlorine down too. That's why the CT you need goes up in winter and spring snowmelt.
